Write whatever Vance should know for this call, in normal words. There is no
format and no required fields. The mission says what to do; this says what you
know and what you want.

Save it as contexts/<mission-name>.txt to have it picked up automatically, or
paste it into the dashboard for a one-off call. Contexts are gitignored because
they usually hold something private.

For a bill negotiation, that might be:

  My name is YOUR NAME and I am the account holder. I authorize Vance to call
  and make decisions for me while I listen.

  My account number is X. My PIN is Y. My postal code is Z. Any other detail
  they might ask for: ...

  I currently pay $X for A, B and C. I want the best deal available. I'd take a
  two-year term if it saves more than $15 a month, but I don't want a hardware
  upgrade and I won't do a credit check. Don't accept anything with a setup
  fee.

For a discovery call, that might be:

  You're calling Jack, who runs a mid-sized construction firm. We met briefly
  last week; he wants project and crew management software built. He's
  frustrated with spreadsheets and thinks his foremen won't use anything
  complicated. Don't quote a price or a timeline — that's mine to decide.

Two things that matter more than they look:

- Say how to pronounce any unusual name, spelled out phonetically. Text to
  speech will otherwise guess, and guess wrong.
- Say what Vance must NOT agree to. That is the one instruction with real
  consequences, and it is the one people forget to write down.
